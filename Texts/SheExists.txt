Breaking into the hard drives of Roisin de Valognes has proven to be a significant challenge. According to our technical experts, the data itself is not conventionally accessible, no identifiable image files can be isolated. Instead, the storage appears to contain only structured noise and seemingly nonsensical computer output.

Profilers have drawn several conclusions about her personality, particularly after analysing the text from her handwritten journal. With this as the only viable lead, the task force proceeded with a high-risk strategy: attempting to lure Roisin, or whatever entity may be operating under that identity into revealing herself by publicly “denying her existence.” Under normal circumstances, this approach should not have worked. It did.

Through coordinated negotiations with multiple local newspapers, later extending into international outlets, the message was distributed globally: that she did not require assistance, and that she had never truly been present.

The result was unintended and widespread. Printing systems across numerous publishing houses began repeatedly producing the same sentence, over and over again, without external input. Our investigation indicates that the attached document represents the earliest recorded instance of this phenomenon.


This first lead the investigation astray, trying to locate the source of such activity always led back different places, Ireland, Estonia, Xiamen, Italy, Sweden, Turkiye...and such jitter, Armenia, Letonia, Luxembourg, Lebanon, Ecuador, Finland and Trinidad.

And then it happened, we got a sign of life...and may have doomed her, for the picture spoke.
